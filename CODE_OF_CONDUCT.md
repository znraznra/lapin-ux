# Code of Conduct

Lapin UX is for people who just want their computer to work, and the community around it should feel the same way: easy to join, nothing to decode. This page says how we treat each other in the project's spaces.

## Our pledge

Everyone who takes part — testers, bug reporters, contributors, people asking a first question — is treated with respect, whatever their age, background, identity, experience, ability, appearance, nationality or beliefs. We want a project where a newcomer is never made to feel stupid for not knowing something.

## What we do

- Be kind and patient, especially with beginners and with people who don't speak English as a first language.
- Explain in plain words. If a reply would need a glossary, rewrite it.
- Disagree about the work, not the person. Critique code, designs and ideas; never the human behind them.
- Give useful, specific feedback, and take feedback with good grace.
- Assume good faith, and say so when you're unsure what someone meant.
- Credit other people's work, including third-party assets and licences (see the README's credits).
- Report real-hardware problems with the details asked for in the bug template, so volunteers' time goes to fixing, not guessing.

## What we don't accept

- Harassment, insults, slurs, name-calling, or put-downs, including "jokes" at someone's expense.
- Discrimination or hate directed at any person or group.
- Threats, intimidation, stalking, or sustained unwanted contact.
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information (address, email, real name when they use a handle, photos) without their consent.
- Pressuring maintainers or other contributors to act on a demand ("fix this now"), or repeatedly reopening decisions that have been explained.
- Spam, trolling, or off-topic promotion.
- Anything else a reasonable person would call unprofessional in a shared workspace.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project is represented: the repository, issues, pull requests and discussions, the wiki, review galleries, chats and any other space run for Lapin UX, and public spaces when someone is clearly speaking for the project.

## Reporting a problem

If someone is breaking this code, or you simply feel unsafe, tell the maintainer privately:

1. Open the repository's **Security** tab and choose **Report a vulnerability**. Title it "Conduct report" and describe what happened, with links if you can. Only the maintainer sees it.
2. If that isn't available, contact the maintainer, [znraznra](https://github.com/znraznra), through the contact details on their GitHub profile.

You don't have to prove anything. Reports are read promptly, kept private, and handled fairly, and the person who reports is never punished for speaking up in good faith. If the report is about the maintainer, say so, and ask a trusted contributor or GitHub support (<https://support.github.com>) to look at it.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides what is fair for each case, normally in this order:

1. **A private note.** A clear explanation of what was wrong and what is expected.
2. **A public warning.** A short, non-personal reminder in the thread, or the comment is edited or hidden.
3. **A temporary ban.** A set period without taking part in the project's spaces.
4. **A permanent ban.** For serious or repeated harm.

Comments, issues and pull requests that break this code may be edited, hidden, locked or deleted without notice. Where it is safe to do so, the reason is explained.

## Maintainers' part

The maintainer applies this code evenly and keeps reports confidential. A conflict of interest means stepping aside from that case.
